Q: The mean deviation about the mean of the rectangular distribution $U(a,b)$ is
A: $\dfrac{b-a}4$
B: $\dfrac{b-a}{2\sqrt3}$
C: $\dfrac{b-a}2$
D: $\dfrac{(b-a)^2}{12}$
ANS: A
S: $|X-\mu|$ is uniform on $\left(0,\tfrac{b-a}2\right)$ by symmetry.
S: Its mean is half of $\tfrac{b-a}2$, i.e. $\tfrac{b-a}4$.
SC: Uniform: MD $=\tfrac{b-a}4$, SD $=\tfrac{b-a}{2\sqrt3}\approx0.289(b-a)$, QD $=\tfrac{b-a}4$ as well. MD/SD $=\tfrac{\sqrt3}2$.
TR: Option B is the standard deviation.
CHK: eq(integrate(abs(x),(x,-R(1,2),R(1,2))), R(1,4))
TIP: MD about the mean of $U(0,1)$ is $\tfrac14$; scale by $b-a$ for any rectangle.
TIP: For symmetric laws the mean and median coincide, so MD about the mean equals the (minimal) MD about the median.

Q: The kurtosis coefficient $\beta_2$ of a rectangular distribution is
A: $1.8$
B: $3$
C: $2.4$
D: $1.2$

SC: Benchmarks: uniform 1.8, triangular 2.4, normal 3, logistic 4.2, Laplace 6, exponential 9.
TR: $2.4$ is the symmetric triangular value.
CHK: (integrate(x**4,(x,-1,1))/2)/(integrate(x**2,(x,-1,1))/2)**2 == R(9,5)

TIP: Central moments of $U(-a,a)$: $\mu_{2r}=\dfrac{a^{2r}}{2r+1}$, odd ones zero.

Q: $K$ is uniformly distributed on $(0,5)$. The probability that the roots of $4x^2+4Kx+K+2=0$ are real is
A: $\tfrac35$
B: $\tfrac25$
C: $\tfrac15$
D: $\tfrac45$
ANS: A
S: Real roots $\iff16K^2-16(K+2)\ge0\iff K^2-K-2\ge0\iff(K-2)(K+1)\ge0$.
S: On $(0,5)$ this means $K\ge2$: probability $\dfrac{5-2}5=\dfrac35$.
SC: Convert the discriminant condition into an interval of $K$, then take the length ratio.
CHK: R(5-2,5) == R(3,5)
TIP: Real roots of $Ax^2+Bx+C$ need $B^2-4AC\ge0$; write it as a condition on the random coefficient.
TIP: Factorising $K^2-K-2=(K-2)(K+1)$ gives the boundary $K=2$.
TIP: Probability = favourable length / total length for a uniform coefficient.

Q: The moment generating function of $U(a,b)$ is
A: $\dfrac{e^{bt}-e^{at}}{(b-a)t}$
B: $\dfrac{e^{bt}+e^{at}}{2}$
C: $\dfrac{\sin\left(\frac{b-a}2t\right)}{\frac{b-a}2t}$
D: $e^{\frac{a+b}2t+\frac{(b-a)^2}{24}t^2}$
ANS: A
S: $M(t)=\dfrac1{b-a}\int_a^be^{tx}dx=\dfrac{e^{bt}-e^{at}}{(b-a)t}$, $t\ne0$ (and $M(0)=1$).
SC: Option C is the <i>characteristic function</i> of the centred uniform $U\left(-\tfrac{b-a}2,\tfrac{b-a}2\right)$.
TIP: At $t=0$ the formula is $\tfrac00$; the limit is 1, as every m.g.f. requires.
TIP: Series: $M(t)=\sum\dfrac{b^{r+1}-a^{r+1}}{(r+1)(b-a)}\dfrac{t^r}{r!}$, so $\mu'_r=\dfrac{b^{r+1}-a^{r+1}}{(r+1)(b-a)}$.
TIP: The c.f. is obtained by replacing $t$ with $it$.

Q: $X$ and $Y$ are independent $U(0,1)$ variates. Then $P\left(X+Y\le\tfrac32\right)$ equals
A: $\tfrac78$
B: $\tfrac34$
C: $\tfrac{15}{16}$
D: $\tfrac12$
ANS: A
S: $Z=X+Y$ has the triangular density $f(z)=z$ on $(0,1]$ and $f(z)=2-z$ on $(1,2)$.
S: $P\left(Z>\tfrac32\right)=\displaystyle\int_{3/2}^2(2-z)\,dz=\tfrac12\left(\tfrac12\right)^2=\tfrac18$.
S: Hence $P\left(Z\le\tfrac32\right)=1-\tfrac18=\tfrac78$.
SC: In the unit square, the region $x+y>\tfrac32$ is a corner triangle with legs $\tfrac12$: area $\tfrac18$.
TR: Option B treats $X+Y$ as uniform on $(0,2)$.
TIP: Sum of two i.i.d. $U(0,1)$ is triangular on $(0,2)$ with peak at 1: mean 1, variance $\tfrac16$.
TIP: For $P(X+Y\le c)$ with $c\le1$ the answer is $\tfrac{c^2}2$; with $1<c<2$ it is $1-\tfrac{(2-c)^2}2$.
TIP: Sum of three i.i.d. uniforms is piecewise quadratic — already bell-shaped (CLT at work).
CHK: integrate(integrate(1,(y,0,R(3,2)-x)),(x,R(1,2),1)) + R(1,2) == R(7,8)

Q: The points of inflexion of the normal curve $N(\mu,\sigma^2)$ are at
A: $\mu\pm\sigma$
B: $\mu\pm2\sigma$
C: $\mu\pm3\sigma$
D: $\mu\pm\tfrac{\sigma}{2}$
ANS: A
S: $f''(x)\propto\left[\dfrac{(x-\mu)^2}{\sigma^2}-1\right]e^{-(x-\mu)^2/2\sigma^2}$.
S: $f''=0\iff(x-\mu)^2=\sigma^2\iff x=\mu\pm\sigma$, where the ordinate is $\dfrac1{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-1/2}$.
SC: Inflexion at one SD — the curve changes from concave to convex there.
CHK: solve(diff(exp(-x**2/2),x,2),x) == [-1,1]
TIP: Height at the inflexion points is $e^{-1/2}\approx0.607$ times the maximum ordinate.
TIP: Between the inflexion points lies $68.27\%$ of the area.
TIP: For $f(x)=ke^{-ax^2+bx}$, inflexion points are at $\mu\pm\tfrac1{\sqrt{2a}}$.
